How this is computed

Leverage Index (Tom Tango, used by FanGraphs) measures how much a moment can move the game. It is the average win-probability swing you should expect from here, divided by the average swing of a typical plate appearance. 1.00 is a perfectly average moment; 2.00 can move the game twice as much.

Two numbers are shown. This at-bat is leverage over the rest of the plate appearance — at 0-0 that is exactly the classic Leverage Index. This pitch is the same idea for the single next pitch, normalised against an average pitch rather than an average plate appearance, so 1.00 again means average.

Both teams are treated as league average, with no home-field edge — the same convention the published LI tables use. Extra innings assume the runner on second.
